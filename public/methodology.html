(X)RUNWAY
Methodology

Every number can be traced.

Each score in (X)RUNWAY traces to three things: what the industry actually teaches, what has actually been measured, and the exact formula printed on the card.

Understanding the score

Here are how the 3 main metrics are defined, tracked, and what it means for you. Please note that the data for these metrics is tracked by the headset only.

PACE
head speed vs the target head speed
score = 100 within ±0.10 m/s of the target; slower costs 90 per m/s, faster costs 45 (40–100). In the band = 100.
CARRIAGE
% of time the head stays level (downward glances negatively affect this score)
score = 40 + 60 × the share of the walk your head stayed level (chin within 7° of neutral) & facing down the line. A head-drop (>15° down) counts against it.
LINE
head alignment with the center line
score = 100 − (your average distance off the centerline, in meters) × 240 (40–100). Dead on the line = 100.
Computed on-device from Meta Quest 3 head tracking only — no camera, no body scan; nothing is scored that the headset did not measure.

The pace evidence

The pace targets come from measurement, not marketing.

  • Measured professional runway cadence: ≈125 steps per minute (per-cluster medians 122.4–129.6), with no significant difference across walk styles or eras.
    Kobayashi, Saito & Murahori, Sensors, June 2024 (PMC11207723) — 69 models' first-look walks, Dolce & Gabbana / Louis Vuitton / Valentino / Versace / Saint Laurent, 1995–2023.
  • No credible source establishes an industry show-music BPM. Every specific figure in circulation traced to vendor, blog, or forum sources and failed adversarial verification.
    docs/RESEARCH-runway-pace-bpm.md — 25 claims verified, 7 refuted.
  • The app's music tempos are derived from the measured cadence, not a claimed industry BPM: 62 · 100 · 125 BPM.
    62 — half time of the emerging level speed · 100 — everyday walking speed or leisure · 125 — emerging level walking speed (industry standard)
  • All published coaching is qualitative — long strides, feet in line, pace to the beat. No source instructs a numeric tempo.
    docs/RESEARCH-runway-instruction.md — 11 findings, 3-vote adversarial verification per claim.
The replay ghost

After a scored walk you can watch a translucent figure replay it. The ghost is drawn from exactly what the headset measured — nothing more.

  • What the ghost shows is measured: your head's recorded path along and across the runway, your speed, and your head pitch (the chin-level channel), sampled at 6 Hz during the walk. The figure moves and tilts its head from that data alone.
  • The figure itself is deliberately abstract. It has no legs, arms, or gait, because the headset does not measure them. State-of-the-art research needs head plus both hand sensors to estimate a body and still calls the problem “vastly underdetermined” (AvatarPoser, ECCV 2022); Meta's own leadership has said leg tracking from a headset alone is “basically not workable… from a physics standpoint”. A walking mannequin here would be invented posture — so we don't draw one.
  • The floor rings mark scored moments. Each ring is placed by the same thresholds the scoring engine itself used (the line gate, the pace band, the head-drop line) — never re-judged after the fact. A clean walk shows no rings.
  • The comparison mode replays two recorded walks (this walk vs your best), started together on the same runway. Both figures are your own recorded data; the growing gap between them is the real pace difference between those two walks.
  • The replay changes nothing. Watching, pausing, or comparing never alters a score or a stored record.
The curriculum

The training syllabus — levels, drills, and advancement — is built the same way the scoring is: every element traces to a verified source, or is labeled as our own design choice. Nothing on the syllabus claims an “industry standard” that cannot be shown.

  • Three source tiers, in order of weight. (1) Named coach and casting practice, quote-verified — the same record the lesson copy is written from (Rocha, Campbell, McKenzie, Joos, CM Models). (2) Peer-reviewed motor-learning research — how practice, feedback, and testing actually build a skill that keeps. (3) Commonly-taught conventions corroborated across commercial guides — used for structure only, named in the fine print below.
  • Advancement reads your cold record, not your best. A level's Mastery Record comes from the first walks of a later session — at least a day apart (our convention), coaching off, the median of three. Published research on motor learning finds delayed, feedback-free performance a better indicator of what was learned than end-of-practice performance — so that is the number that advances you. Your in-session best is still shown; it just doesn't gate.
  • What can gate, and what never can. Gates reference only channels the headset measures and you can see, with the threshold printed up front. No seat time, no composites, no unmeasured skill (feet, hips, arms, expression), and nothing worded as a career outcome — ever.
  • The unmeasured half of the craft is taught, labeled. Foot placement, posture, arms, expression are coached in copy at every level and marked “coached, never scored.” A mirror, a video, and a human coach see what this headset cannot — the syllabus says so on its face.
  • Progress records, not credentials. This product issues no certificates and implies no placement. Consumer-protection guidance on modeling schools names pay-for-credential and promised work as the warning signs — the syllabus is designed to be the opposite of that pattern.
  • Claims are dated and scoped. Where the record rests on a survey, the copy says so — e.g. “no runway-walking course in the catalogs we checked (FIT, LCF short courses, July 2026)”, never “no fashion college teaches walking.”
  • Method, in one line: every factual claim behind the syllabus was checked against its primary source before shipping; contested evidence is flagged as contested, and refuted claims are recorded in the research file and never cited.

Fine print — structural patterns referenced, with no affiliation and no endorsement implied or claimed: the graded-exam structure (stable criteria across levels, criterion-referenced marking, outcome-only assessment) follows the pattern documented in ABRSM's published exam guides; the tape-line home-drill structure adapts a routine published by a commercial modeling school (John Casablancas International — structure only; its coaching cues are neither borrowed nor scored); competency-progression language follows the Aurora Institute's published definition; the consumer-protection markers are the FTC's published guidance on modeling schools.

Reproducible scoring

A score you can defend is a score that can be reproduced.

  • The scoring engine is pinned by 18 locked vector families: recorded walks replayed through the production code must reproduce their scores byte-for-byte.
  • The iOS companion replays the identical vectors — the same walk scores the same on both platforms.
  • Any change to scoring or coach copy regenerates the vectors in a reviewed, committed step. Scores cannot drift silently.
  • The exact formula behind every displayed score is printed beside it (the section above).
Data & privacy

Everything stays on your headset: no account, no server, no camera images. Your walks are yours — stored locally, exported only when you choose.

  • Scoring input is the headset's head-tracking pose only (position + rotation).
  • The camera passthrough never reaches the app — WebXR exposes no camera pixels to it.
  • Walk history lives in the headset browser's local storage. No account, no server, no analytics.
  • Exports (CSV / Excel) are files the trainee downloads to their own device, on their own action.
